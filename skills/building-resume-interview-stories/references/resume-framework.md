# Resume Framework

Use this reference after the business model and evidence ledger are stable.

## 1. Define the Target and Screening Rubric

Treat the JD as the question and the resume as the answer. Extract:

- official role title and hiring context;
- primary responsibilities and business problems;
- required capabilities, tools, and domain signals;
- hard constraints such as degree, graduation date, location, or language;
- evidence the recruiter is likely to accept for each requirement.

Build this internal map before rewriting:

| Priority JD requirement | Strongest Confirmed evidence | Resume location | Gap or risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use one target role per resume version. If the user provides no JD, confirm the role family or state the assumptions used for a general review.

## 2. Pass the Ten-Second Screen

The first scan should reveal, without inference:

- target role and contact path;
- school, degree, major, and graduation timing;
- company/project names, roles, and a coherent timeline;
- the most relevant capability and result;
- recognizable terms from the JD, backed by evidence rather than keyword stuffing.

Make the recruiter choose between clear evidence, not reconstruct the candidate's story. Remove redundant headings such as `个人简历` and low-signal details that compete with stronger proof.

When reviewing an existing resume, prioritize findings before rewriting:

1. **Must fix:** unsupported claims, target mismatch, missing hard constraints, broken chronology, unreadable structure, or export defects.
2. **Evidence gap:** ownership, result, scope, or examples that need confirmation before stronger wording is safe.
3. **Polish:** wording, section order, density, alignment, and restrained visual emphasis.

Return the smallest set of changes that materially improves screening. Do not bury the user in cosmetic comments while a must-fix problem remains.

## 3. Select the Sections

Use only sections that help the target-role decision. A common early-career order is:

1. name, contact, and target role;
2. education;
3. internship/work and selected projects;
4. relevant skills, certifications, awards, or coursework.

Add a summary only when it gives an evidence-backed positioning statement. Do not default to photos, party membership, ethnicity, age, gender, or other sensitive identity details; include them only when an explicit requirement, applicable locale, and the user's informed choice justify them.

## 4. Choose the Narrative

Write the experience around one central value proposition, not a list of every task.

Examples of valid narratives:

- turn ambiguous requests into standardized capability;
- replace repeated manual work with an automated workflow;
- improve data or operational reliability;
- scale a successful approach across teams, products, or scenarios;
- convert analysis into a decision, launch, or measurable improvement.

Choose the narrative that is both relevant to the target role and supported by evidence. Relevance cannot override truth.

## 5. Use Implicit STAR

| Element | Resume expression |
| --- | --- |
| Situation | Short problem context, usually in the first bullet |
| Task | The candidate's mandate or responsibility |
| Action | Specific decisions, process, analysis, implementation, or collaboration |
| Result | Measured outcome or verifiable change in capability/workflow |

Do not label bullets `S`, `T`, `A`, and `R` unless the user explicitly asks for a teaching version.

## 6. Default Bullet Architecture

Use 3-5 bullets. A useful five-part pattern is:

1. **需求拆解/问题定义：** Who needed what, and how the candidate clarified it.
2. **方案执行/能力建设：** What workflow, analysis, product, or system was delivered.
3. **质量监控/风险控制：** How correctness, consistency, compliance, or reliability was protected.
4. **扩展复用/协同推进：** How the approach expanded or became reusable.
5. **项目成果：** What changed for users, the process, or the organization.

Merge sections when the experience is narrow. Do not pad to five bullets.

## 7. Lead With Business, Support With Technique

Weak:

> Used Python, Pandas, NumPy, and HDF5 to process data and build matrices.

Stronger:

> **流程自动化：** 针对研究数据依赖人工清洗和转换的问题，使用 Python/Pandas 搭建标准化处理原型，将原始数据转化为可直接实验的输入，减少重复准备环节。

The stronger version explains the problem and value; tools prove execution.

## 8. Calibrate Verbs

| Avoid unless proven | Conservative alternative |
| --- | --- |
| led / drove | participated in, supported, coordinated |
| designed the platform | participated in designing the workflow/prototype |
| launched to production | delivered a prototype, completed internal validation |
| improved efficiency by X% | reduced repeated manual steps, shortened preparation workflow |
| guaranteed accuracy | added controls, reduced risk, improved consistency |

## 9. State Results Without Fabricating Numbers

When metrics exist, state baseline, change, unit, period, and ownership.

When metrics do not exist, use verifiable result types:

- delivered a reusable prototype, report, dashboard, model, playbook, or workflow;
- changed a process from manual preparation to rule-based generation;
- expanded coverage across teams, markets, datasets, reports, or scenarios;
- supported research, decision-making, launch, review, or downstream operations;
- established validation, monitoring, documentation, or repeatability.

Do not replace a missing metric with vague hype such as “significantly,” “greatly,” or “industry-leading.”

## 10. Produce Targeted Variants Only When Useful

- **HR/business version:** problem, user, action, value, collaboration.
- **Technical version:** architecture, core implementation, validation, scale, tradeoffs.
- **Product version:** user need, requirement abstraction, capability design, iteration, outcome.

Keep the factual core identical across variants.

## 11. Run Delivery and Layout QA

- Default to one A4 page for early-career candidates only when meaningful content remains readable; do not compress text to obey a page-count ritual.
- Keep section hierarchy, field order, dates, punctuation, spacing, and alignment consistent.
- Use restrained typography and color. One or two fonts and a small neutral palette are safe heuristics, not mandatory design targets.
- Keep emphasis scarce: prioritize section labels and verified results instead of bolding every keyword.
- Avoid decorative fonts, italics, dense blocks, high-saturation color fields, and copied templates that obscure information.
- Follow the submission portal's format requirements. When PDF is accepted, preview the final export and remove accidental blank pages.
- Use a descriptive filename such as `Name_School_TargetRole.pdf` when the receiver has not specified a naming rule.
- If automated parsing matters, verify that the exported text remains selectable/extractable and that section labels and chronology survive parsing.

Exact fonts, point sizes, colors, and shape dimensions from a sample template are examples only. Optimize for clarity, system compatibility, and the actual content.

## 12. Resume Quality Checklist

- Does every high-priority JD requirement map to evidence or a visible gap?
- Can a non-specialist explain what the candidate did after one read?
- Can a recruiter identify the target, education, timeline, and strongest proof in ten seconds?
- Does every bullet begin with a problem, action, or result rather than a tool name?
- Is personal ownership accurate?
- Is there at least one explicit result?
- Are prototype, validation, adoption, and production clearly distinguished?
- Does each technical term earn its place?
- Can the candidate defend every noun and verb in an interview?
- Is the final file readable, consistently formatted, correctly named, and free of blank pages?
